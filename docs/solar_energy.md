# Solar Energy Basics

Photovoltaic (PV) cells convert sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor materials, most commonly silicon. When photons hit the cell, they knock electrons loose, creating a flow of current.

Typical residential solar panels have an efficiency between 15 and 22 percent. Efficiency drops slightly as temperature rises, so panels in very hot climates produce a little less than their rated output.

A standard rooftop installation has a lifespan of about 25 to 30 years. Most manufacturers guarantee at least 80 percent of original output after 25 years. Inverters usually need replacement after 10 to 15 years.

Net metering lets households send surplus electricity back to the grid and receive credit on their bill. Rules vary by state and utility.
